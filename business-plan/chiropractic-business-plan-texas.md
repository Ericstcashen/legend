## 1. Executive Summary

Lone Star Spine & Wellness is a single-doctor chiropractic clinic opening in a
high-growth Texas suburb, treating musculoskeletal complaints (low-back pain,
neck pain, headaches, sports and auto-injury cases) with spinal manipulation,
rehabilitative exercise, and adjunct soft-tissue therapy. The practice competes
on **access** (same-week appointments, evening hours), **outcomes** (measurable
functional improvement, not indefinite "maintenance" plans), and a **transparent
cash-plus-insurance** payment model that removes the pricing anxiety patients
associate with chiropractic.

- **Ask / capital required:** ~$150,000 startup (SBA 7(a) or equipment-secured
  term loan + owner injection), plus ~$60,000 personal runway to cover the
  owner's living costs through month ~9.
- **Break-even:** roughly **28–34 patient visits per week** at a blended
  ~$70 net revenue per visit covering fixed overhead; targeted by **month 9–12**.
- **Year 3 target:** ~55–70 visits/week, ~$430–520K gross revenue, 18–24%
  owner pre-tax margin after paying the owner a clinical salary.
- **Why now:** Texas is the fastest-growing large state; suburban corridors add
  rooftops faster than healthcare supply. Chiropractic demand tracks population,
  desk-work posture complaints, and an aging-but-active population.

The core risk is the same one that sinks most new clinics: **too few new
patients per month at too high a fixed cost.** The plan is built around keeping
fixed cost low until new-patient volume is proven, then scaling deliberately.

### 2.3 Legal & regulatory structure (Texas specifics)
- **Licensure:** The doctor must hold an active Texas chiropractic license from
  the **Texas Board of Chiropractic Examiners (TBCE)**. Verify current renewal
  cycle, continuing-education hours, and jurisprudence requirements directly
  with TBCE.
- **Scope of practice:** Texas chiropractic scope is defined by statute and TBCE
  rules and has been the subject of litigation (notably over needle EMG/VONT and
  the definition of the "musculoskeletal system"). **Do not build revenue lines
  around any procedure whose scope status is unsettled** — confirm current,
  final rules before offering it.
- **Entity:** Texas requires professional practices to organize as a
  **Professional Association (PA)** or **Professional LLC (PLLC)**; owners must
  be licensed. File with the Texas Secretary of State; adopt an S-corp tax
  election once profit justifies reasonable-salary planning (CPA decision).
- **Corporate practice / ownership:** Keep ownership compliant — non-licensee
  ownership of a professional entity is restricted. If you partner with an
  investor or a massage/PT business, structure it through an MSO/management
  agreement reviewed by a Texas healthcare attorney, not shared clinical
  ownership.
- **Registrations & compliance:** NPI number, Texas sales-and-use tax permit
  (for retail supplement/pillow sales), CLIA waiver only if doing in-house lab
  (usually N/A), OSHA and HIPAA compliance program, radiology registration with
  the Texas Department of State Health Services if operating an on-site X-ray
  unit (many startups **refer out imaging** to avoid this capital + regulatory
  load initially).
- **Insurance:** Malpractice (professional liability) — chiropractic premiums in
  Texas are relatively low (~$1,500–$3,500/yr typical); general liability;
  business property; workers' comp once you have employees; cyber/HIPAA breach.

## 3. Market Analysis

### 3.1 Industry
Chiropractic is a mature, fragmented, largely owner-operated healthcare segment.
Nationally it's a multi-billion-dollar market growing at low-to-mid single
digits. The economics are attractive for a disciplined operator because fixed
costs are modest, gross margins on the core service are high, and demand is
recurring. The failure mode is not lack of demand — it's under-marketed clinics
that never reach visit-volume break-even before the owner's runway ends.

### 3.2 Why Texas
- **Population growth:** Texas leads U.S. states in absolute population growth;
  suburban rings around Austin, DFW, Houston, and San Antonio are adding
  households and healthcare demand faster than provider supply.
- **Business climate:** No state personal income tax (note: the Texas
  **franchise/margin tax** still applies to the entity above a revenue
  threshold — confirm the current no-tax-due threshold with your CPA).
- **Auto-injury volume:** Texas traffic density and MVA rates generate a steady
  personal-injury (PI) referral stream, though PI collections are slow and
  attorney-dependent — treat as upside, not base case.
- **Active demographics:** High youth-sports participation and a large
  desk-working professional base drive musculoskeletal demand.

### 3.4 Competitive landscape
| Competitor type | Strength | Exploitable weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Legacy solo DC (20+ yrs) | Loyal base, referrals | Dated space, limited hours, no online booking |
| Corporate/franchise adjustment clubs (membership model) | Price, brand, walk-ins | Rushed visits, no rehab, no doctor continuity |
| PT clinics | Insurance networks, rehab | Can't adjust; longer episodes of care |
| Multi-disciplinary PI mills | Volume, attorney pipelines | Reputation risk, insurance scrutiny |

**Positioning:** "Doctor-led, outcomes-focused, honest plans, easy access." Not
the cheapest; not a subscription mill; not an endless-maintenance shop.

### 3.5 Target patient segments
1. **Working adults 30–55** with mechanical low-back/neck pain (core, insurance + cash).
2. **Active/older adults 55+** managing arthritis-related stiffness and mobility.
3. **Athletes & weekend warriors** (sports injury, performance) — cash-friendly.
4. **Auto-injury (PI)** — upside line, managed conservatively.
5. **Prenatal/postpartum & pediatric** — only if you have credible training; strong referral loop with OBs/midwives.

## 4. Marketing & Sales Strategy

### 4.1 Positioning & brand
Clean, modern, trustworthy — closer to a boutique medical/wellness office than a
1990s "subluxation" clinic. Emphasize measurable outcomes, defined treatment
plans with an endpoint, and transparent pricing.

### 4.2 Patient acquisition channels (ranked by ROI for a startup)
1. **Google Business Profile + local SEO + reviews.** Non-negotiable. Most new
   patients search "chiropractor near me." Systematize review requests (aim
   50+ reviews at 4.8★ in year one).
2. **Google Local Services / paid search** for high-intent keywords; tight
   geo-radius; track cost-per-new-patient (target < $60–90 CPA).
3. **Referral partnerships:** primary-care MDs, PTs, orthopedists, gyms/CrossFit
   boxes, running clubs, massage therapists, OBs/midwives. This is the durable
   moat — build it doctor-to-doctor.
4. **Employer/corporate wellness** with nearby office parks and warehouses
   (posture/ergonomics lunch-and-learns, injury-prevention).
5. **Community presence:** youth sports sponsorships, 5Ks, local Facebook groups.
6. **Website:** fast, mobile-first, online booking, insurance-check tool, patient
   education content. New-patient special (compliant, no illegal inducements —
   confirm with TBCE advertising rules; avoid anything that looks like an
   unlawful insurance co-pay waiver).

### 4.3 Offers & conversion
- Straightforward new-patient exam offer (compliant with Texas advertising and
  insurance rules — **no blanket co-pay/deductible waivers**, which can be
  illegal).
- On-the-spot report-of-findings that presents a **finite** care plan with a
  re-evaluation checkpoint, not a scare-tactic multi-year contract.
- Optional cash **membership/wellness plan** for maintenance patients (must be
  structured to avoid dual-fee/insurance-fraud issues — CPA + attorney review).

### 4.4 Retention
Recall systems, automated re-exam reminders, home-exercise plans, email
newsletter, and outcome tracking (e.g., Oswestry/Neck Disability Index) that
lets patients *see* progress. Referral > paid: every satisfied patient asked at
the right moment.

## 5. Operations Plan

### 5.1 Location & facility
- **Size:** ~1,200–1,800 sq ft — reception, 2–3 treatment rooms, one open rehab
  bay, restroom, small office.
- **Lease:** target $22–34/sq ft/yr NNN in a visible retail/medical strip with
  parking and signage; negotiate **free rent build-out period (2–4 months)** and
  a modest TI (tenant-improvement) allowance.
- **Buildout:** keep light — most chiropractic buildout is cosmetic. Budget
  $15–40K depending on plumbing/room count.

### 5.2 Equipment (startup capital list)
| Item | Est. cost |
|---|---|
| Adjusting table(s) — 1 new + 1 used | $6,000–12,000 |
| Drop/flexion-distraction table | $3,000–6,000 |
| Therapy: e-stim, ultrasound, laser (optional) | $3,000–8,000 |
| Rehab: bands, TRX, stability, cable column | $2,500 |
| EHR + billing software (annual, see below) | $3,000–7,200/yr |
| Front-desk / computers / phones / POS | $4,000 |
| Signage & branding | $4,000–8,000 |
| Buildout / furniture / decor | $15,000–40,000 |
| Initial supplies & inventory | $3,000 |
| **Equipment/setup subtotal** | **~$45,000–90,000** |

> **Imaging decision:** Do **not** buy an X-ray suite at launch (capital +
> Texas radiology registration + maintenance). Refer imaging to a local
> radiology center; bring it in-house only once volume justifies it.

### 5.3 Technology stack
- **EHR/practice management:** chiropractic-specific (e.g., a SOAP-note +
  scheduling + billing platform). Prioritize clean billing, online scheduling,
  automated recalls, and reporting.
- **Billing:** in-house biller part-time vs. outsourced clearinghouse/RCM
  service (~4–7% of collections). Start outsourced to avoid a hire.
- **Payments:** card-on-file, HSA/FSA acceptance, transparent superbills for
  out-of-network patients.

### 5.4 Staffing plan
| Phase | Team | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Launch (mo 1–6) | Owner-DC + 1 front desk (FT or 2 PT) | Keep payroll minimal; owner does clinical + marketing |
| Growth (mo 7–18) | + 1 chiro assistant/rehab tech | Offload therapies/modalities, protect DC's adjusting time |
| Scale (yr 2–3) | + associate DC or massage LMT, add front-desk hours | Extend capacity/hours without owner burnout |
- Texas employment: workers' comp (Texas is a non-mandatory WC state but strongly
  advised for a clinic), payroll via a service, and clear roles. Independent-
  contractor classification of associate DCs is scrutinized — confirm with CPA.

### 5.5 Hours & capacity
- Offer at least two evening blocks and one early-morning block weekly — access
  is a differentiator vs. legacy 9–5 clinics.
- Practical solo-DC capacity: ~8–14 visits/day sustainably; a well-run day with
  a rehab tech supports ~20–25.

### 5.6 Clinical & compliance protocols
- Standardized new-patient workup, outcome-measure documentation, informed
  consent (including cervical manipulation risk disclosure), medical-necessity
  documentation for insurance, and defined discharge criteria.
- HIPAA program, OSHA bloodborne/hazard plan, medical-record retention per Texas
  rules, and a compliant advertising review checklist.

Year 1 runs at a loss — expected and funded by the runway and loan. Profitability
follows visit volume, and visit volume follows **new-patient marketing
consistency**. The single most important operating metric is **new patients per
month**; track it weekly.

### 7.6 Key metrics dashboard (review weekly/monthly)
- New patients/month (leading indicator #1)
- Cost per acquired new patient (target < $60–90)
- Patient visit average (PVA) — visits per care episode
- Collections rate (% of billed actually collected; target > 92% on insurance)
- Days in A/R (target < 40)
- Visits/week and revenue/visit
- Rebooking rate and 90-day reactivation

## 9. Risk Analysis & Mitigation
| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Slow new-patient ramp | Cash burn, insolvency | Keep fixed cost low; runway funded; marketing spend tied to CPA; don't over-hire |
| Reimbursement cuts / denials | Margin compression | Diversify to cash/membership; strong documentation; RCM partner; audit-proof notes |
| Regulatory/scope change (TBCE) | Lost revenue line | Only build on settled scope; monitor TBCE rulemaking; attorney on retainer |
| PI collections risk | A/R tied up 12–24 mo | Cap PI at ~8–10% of book; vet attorneys; don't finance the practice on liens |
| Owner illness/injury | Revenue stops (solo) | Disability insurance; cross-train assistant; associate DC by yr 2 |
| Compliance misstep (advertising, dual-fee, co-pay waiver) | Fines, license risk | Compliance checklist; attorney review of all offers; no illegal inducements |
| Local competition / franchise price war | Volume pressure | Compete on outcomes/access/continuity, not price |
| Lease/location underperforms | Fixed-cost trap | Negotiate exit/co-tenancy clauses; validate foot traffic before signing |
